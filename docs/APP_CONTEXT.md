# HisaabMate / BorrowLedger — Application Context

This document is the working context for understanding the current app before changing features, terminology, data structures, or flows.

It was written from the implementation currently in the repository on 27 September 2026. The code is the source of truth; some sections of `README.md` describe an older version of the product.

## 1. Product identity and purpose

- Repository/package identity: `BorrowLedger` / `borrow_ledger`.
- User-facing app name: `HisaabMate`.
- Current slogan: `Track • Split • Settle`.
- Product purpose: locally track money given to or received from people, item/service credit (`udhari`), personal expenses, two-person expenses with someone, group expenses, and later settlements.
- Storage: local SQLite database. There is no backend, account system, or cloud synchronization in the current implementation.
- Current app version constant: `1.0.0`.
- Supported languages: English, Hindi, and Marathi.

The central product concept is a contact balance. A contact may have direct cash/udhari transactions, shared-spend transactions, split-generated balances, and settlement history. The app calculates what the user should receive from or pay to that contact.

## 2. Current navigation and entry points

The main shell is `lib/presentation/screens/main_screen.dart`.

The bottom navigation currently has three tabs:

1. **Home** — `MergedBorrowLendScreen`.
2. **Group Expenses** — `SplitsScreen`.
3. **My Expenses** — `MergedExpensesScreen`.

Home contains two modes:

- **People**: contact summaries, balances, category counts, and contact-specific activity.
- **Ledger**: all direct/shared/split activity with search, category filters, type filters, and sorting.

The Home screen has the primary floating action button. It opens `showAddTransactionMenu` with four choices:

| Current choice | Internal route | What it currently creates |
|---|---|---|
| Money Transaction | `AddTransactionScreen(category: cash)` | One direct cash borrow/lend transaction |
| Udhari | `AddTransactionScreen(category: udhari)` | One item/service credit transaction |
| Expense with Someone | `AddTransactionScreen(category: shared_spend)` | One two-person shared-expense transaction |
| Group Expense | `AddSplitScreen` | One multi-person group expense with bills and participants |

The Splits and Expenses tabs each also have their own add button. Contact pages can open the same add menu with a contact preselected.

## 3. Core vocabulary and ledger semantics

### 3.1 `type`

`TransactionModel.type` is one of:

- `lend`: the user is the creditor; the contact owes the user.
- `borrow`: the user is the debtor; the user owes the contact.

For ordinary cash and udhari records, this directly describes the transaction direction.

For shared spend, `type` is derived from who paid:

- User paid → `lend` because the contact owes the user's share.
- Contact paid → `borrow` because the user owes their own share.

### 3.2 `category`

The main transaction categories are:

- `cash`: direct money given or received.
- `udhari`: an item or service was given/taken on credit.
- `shared_spend`: a two-person shared expense where one person paid.
- `split`: a transaction generated from a richer group split.

Personal expenses use a separate `expenses` table and do not have a contact balance.

### 3.3 What `amount` means

For `cash` and `udhari`, `amount` is the amount of the direct transaction.

For `shared_spend`, `amount` is the amount owed by the counterparty after the split, not necessarily the full bill:

- User paid: `amount` = contact's share.
- Contact paid: `amount` = user's share.

The full bill and both shares are also stored in the shared-spend metadata fields. This is important when changing labels or migrating the feature: shared-spend records should not be treated as ordinary full-bill expenses.

For `split` generated transactions, `amount` is the pending amount for one contact route generated by the split settlement calculator.

## 4. Add menu and form behavior

### 4.1 Shared form shell

`AddTransactionScreen` is reused for cash, udhari, and shared spend. It has these broad sections:

1. Selected transaction kind card.
2. Person/contact picker.
3. Direction/payer control.
4. Details.
5. Outcome preview.
6. Date.
7. Optional expected return date for cash/udhari.
8. Optional note for cash/udhari.

The contact can be selected from phone contacts or entered manually. Manual contacts are deduplicated by normalized phone number, or by name when no phone is available.

### 4.2 Money Transaction (`cash`)

Current fields and behavior:

- Contact: required.
- Direction: `I gave` / `I got`.
- Amount: required and positive.
- Transaction date: required/defaults to today.
- Expected return date: optional.
- Note/description: optional.

This is for direct money movement, such as lending ₹1,000 or borrowing ₹500.

### 4.3 Udhari (`udhari`)

Current user-facing wording:

- Title: **Udhari**.
- Description: “Items or services bought or sold on credit.”

Current fields and behavior:

- Contact: required.
- Direction: `I gave item` / `I took item`.
- Item/service name: required.
- Quantity: optional.
- Amount: required and positive.
- Transaction date: required/defaults to today.
- Expected return date: optional.
- Note/description: optional.

The item and quantity values feed suggestion/history tables (`udhari_items` and `udhari_quantities`). This feature is meant for item/service credit, not for a friend paying a shared bill.

### 4.4 Expense with Someone (`shared_spend`)

Current user-facing wording:

- Title: **Expense with Someone**.
- Description: “You paid for them or they paid for you.”
- Form description: “Record a payment made for you or by you.”
- Direction section: **Who paid?**
- Payer choices: **I paid for them** / **[Person] paid for me**.
- Cost mode: **We shared the cost** is off by default for new entries.

Current fields:

- Contact: required; exactly one other person.
- What it was for: required, stored in the transaction description.
- Default paid-on-behalf mode: one amount paid for the other person.
- Optional shared-cost mode: total bill, `Split equally`, and the counterparty's share.
- Transaction date: required.
- No expected return date.

Paid-on-behalf calculation:

- User paid: the contact owes the user the full entered amount; saved type = `lend`.
- Contact paid: the user owes the contact the full entered amount; saved type = `borrow`.
- New paid-on-behalf rows encode the full amount in `shared_total_amount` and use a zero/full share pair to remain compatible with existing fields.

Shared-cost calculation:

- Equal mode sets the share to half of the full bill.
- User paid: user share = total bill − contact share; contact share = entered amount; saved type = `lend`.
- Contact paid: contact share = total bill − user's share; user share = entered amount; saved type = `borrow`.

Current persistence:

- Saved in `transactions`.
- `transaction_category = shared_spend`.
- `source_type = shared_spend`; `source_id` is not currently populated.
- `shared_total_amount` stores the full bill.
- `shared_user_share` and `shared_contact_share` store both sides.
- `shared_paid_by_user` stores who paid.
- The purpose is saved as `description` and also recorded in `shared_spend_purposes` for suggestions.
- A derived presentation classifier identifies paid-on-behalf, shared-cost, and legacy ambiguous rows without adding a database mode column.

Meaning of the user's intended example:

> A friend buys something for the user at a mall or pays the user's mess contribution.

This is correctly represented as a shared-spend-style balance when the record means “the friend paid on my behalf.” It is not an item-credit record and does not need to be called a cash loan. The resulting balance is simply: **I owe this person because they paid for me**.

Important boundary: this is a two-person flow. It supports one contact and one purpose. It defaults to a simple paid-on-behalf entry, with shared-cost allocation revealed only when needed. It is not a group expense workspace.

### 4.5 Group Expense (`split`)

`AddSplitScreen` is a separate, richer workflow.

Top-level fields:

- Split title: required.
- Date: required.
- Optional note/description.

Bills:

- At least one bill is required.
- Each bill has a name and amount.
- Each bill can be paid by the user or by one of the added participants.
- Multiple bills can be added, such as hotel, dinner, and fuel.
- The bill list is the source for the total amount and who paid what.

Participants:

- At least one participant is required.
- Participants are selected from contacts.
- Duplicate participant contacts are prevented.
- The participant card shows their share and the amount they paid during the bill.

Split method:

- **Split equally**: each participant gets an equal share; the user's share is the remaining share of the total.
- Manual mode: participant shares can be entered individually; the user's share is total minus participant shares.

Settlement-route option:

- With at least two participants, the app can use an optimized route or a mediator route.
- This controls how the pending balances are routed and previewed; it does not change the original bill or share data.

Validation requires:

- At least one participant.
- At least one bill.
- Total paid across the user and participants must exactly cover the bill total.
- Manual participant shares cannot exceed the total.

This feature is therefore best understood as **group expense allocation and settlement**, not merely “someone paid for me.” It can represent the user's example, but it is heavier than necessary for a single friend and a single personal payment.

## 5. Expense with Someone versus Group Expense — current product distinction

The implementation has a real domain distinction, even though the labels look similar:

| Dimension | Expense with Someone | Group Expense |
|---|---|---|
| People | User + one contact | User + one or more participants |
| Bills | One implicit payment or optional shared bill | One or many explicit bills |
| Payer | User or the one contact | User or any participant per bill |
| Share calculation | Paid-on-behalf amount or optional equal/custom two-person share | Equal/manual multi-person shares |
| Storage root | One `transactions` row | `split_expenses` + bills + participants |
| Balance entry | The transaction itself | Generated `category = split` transactions |
| Detail workspace | Transaction details | Split details, participant settlement, invoice sharing |
| Best use | “Alex paid my share” or “I paid Alex's share” | “Trip/dinner with several people” |

The user-facing problem was primarily naming and entry-point design, not that the two implementations are identical. They both expose payer + sharing because both need to answer the same accounting question, but Group Expense has substantially more structure and lifecycle.

Final terminology direction:

- Treat **Split** as a calculation/action, not as a separate reason for owing.
- Use **Expense with Someone** for the simple two-person flow.
- Make paid-on-behalf the default flow; reveal shared-cost fields only when **We shared the cost** is enabled.
- Use **Group Expense** for multiple participants, multiple bills, and detailed settlement.
- Keep **My Expenses** separate because personal expenses do not affect contact balances.
- Use “What do you want to add?” as the add-menu heading without merging the underlying models.

Do not rename database categories casually. `shared_spend` and `split` are already used by filters, summaries, transaction rendering, sync, and backups.

## 6. Data model and persistence

### 6.1 Contacts

Table: `contacts`.

Stores name, phone, email, UPI ID, avatar, and timestamps. Contacts are referenced by direct transactions, split participants, bills paid by a contact, and settlement records.

Main code:

- Model: `lib/data/models/contact_model.dart`
- Repository: `lib/data/repositories/contact_repository.dart`
- Cubit: `lib/presentation/cubit/contact_cubit.dart`
- Screens: `contact_picker_screen.dart`, `contact_edit_screen.dart`, `contact_wise_transactions_screen.dart`

### 6.2 Direct/shared transaction ledger

Table: `transactions`.

Important fields:

- `type`, `transaction_category`, `contact_id`, `amount`.
- `description`, `date`, `created_at`, `updated_at`.
- Udhari fields: `item_name`, `quantity`, `expected_date`, `paid_amount`.
- Settlement/source fields: `is_settlement`, `source_type`, `source_id`.
- Shared fields: `shared_total_amount`, `shared_user_share`, `shared_contact_share`, `shared_paid_by_user`.

Main code:

- Model: `lib/data/models/transaction_model.dart`
- Repository: `lib/data/repositories/transaction_repository.dart`
- Cubit: `lib/presentation/cubit/borrow_lend_cubit.dart`
- Entry form: `lib/presentation/screens/add_transaction_screen.dart`
- Shared-expense mode compatibility: `lib/core/utils/shared_expense_mode.dart`
- Display/detail: `transaction_list_item.dart`, `transaction_details_screen.dart`

### 6.3 Personal expenses

Table: `expenses`.

`ExpenseModel` contains amount, category, description, date, and timestamps. The ten current categories are Food & Dining, Transportation, Shopping, Entertainment, Bills & Utilities, Healthcare, Education, Travel, Groceries, and Others.

Personal expenses are intentionally separate from contact balances and split settlement. They are used for spending summaries, category breakdowns, monthly trends, search, and filters.

Main code:

- Model: `lib/data/models/expense_model.dart`
- Repository: `lib/data/repositories/expense_repository.dart`
- Cubit: `lib/presentation/cubit/expense_cubit.dart`
- Screens: `expenses_screen.dart`, `add_expense_screen.dart`

### 6.4 Group splits

Tables:

- `split_expenses`: title, total amount, amount paid by user, status, settlement route, mediator, timestamps.
- `split_bills`: bill title, amount, payer, date, note.
- `split_participants`: contact, share amount, amount paid during the bill, later paid amount, status.

Models:

- `SplitExpenseModel`
- `SplitBillModel`
- `SplitParticipantModel`

Main code:

- Models: `lib/data/models/split_model.dart`
- Repository: `lib/data/repositories/split_repository.dart`
- Cubit: `lib/presentation/cubit/split_cubit.dart`
- Create/edit: `add_split_screen.dart`
- Detail/settlement/invoice: `split_detail_screen.dart`
- Calculation: `core/utils/split_settlement_calculator.dart`

### 6.5 Settlement records

Tables:

- `contact_settlements`: records a full or partial net settlement with a contact, including method and payment reference.
- `contact_settlement_effects`: records which direct transactions, split participants, or split balances were affected.

Settlement history is kept visible. A settlement clears balance calculations without erasing the historical activity.

The contact settlement flow can combine direct balances and split balances and calculate a net amount. It supports manual and UPI-oriented flows, partial settlement, full settlement, and settlement details.

## 7. Split-to-ledger synchronization

The split object is the source of truth for group splits. `SplitTransactionSyncHelper` translates pending split routes into generated rows in `transactions`.

Current synchronization behavior:

1. Load split, participants, and bills.
2. Delete prior generated transactions where `source_type = split` and `source_id = split id`.
3. Calculate pending routes using `SplitSettlementCalculator`.
4. For routes affecting the user, create a transaction for the relevant contact.
5. Use `category = split`, `source_type = split`, and `source_id = split id`.
6. If there are no pending routes, mark the split settled; otherwise keep it pending.

Generated rows must not be edited as if they were standalone direct transactions. Editing the split should regenerate them. Settling or deleting a split removes its generated transaction rows while preserving settlement/history records as designed by the settlement flow.

Main code: `lib/data/repositories/split_transaction_sync_helper.dart` and the sync calls in `split_repository.dart`.

## 8. Settlement and balance behavior

Balances can contain multiple sources:

- Direct cash transactions.
- Udhari transactions.
- Shared-spend transactions.
- Split-generated transactions.
- Settlement records/effects.

The Home/People view summarizes the contact-level result. Contact detail shows a unified activity stream, category filters, split history, settlement history, partial/full settlement actions, and UPI actions.

Split detail additionally supports:

- Per-participant settlement.
- Full split settlement.
- Partial payments.
- Settlement route preview.
- Split invoice image generation and sharing.

When introducing a new expense type, the key design question is not only “what screen records it?” but also “which contact balance source should it contribute to?”

## 11. Settings and data management

The settings drawer currently provides:

- Language selection.
- Light/dark/system theme selection.
- User profile/share name.
- UPI ID setup.
- Export backup.
- Import/restore backup.
- Clear all data.
- App information.

Backup/restore includes contacts, transactions, expenses, split expenses, split participants, split bills, suggestion tables, user profile data, and settlement data. Backup format/version handling includes compatibility logic for older backups and older split data without bill rows.

All data is local. Clearing data deletes all main records, suggestions, split data, and settlement data from SQLite and clears stored avatars through the settings flow.

## 12. Localization and naming notes

Localization files are:

- `lib/l10n/app_en.arb`
- `lib/l10n/app_hi.arb`
- `lib/l10n/app_mr.arb`

The user-facing names are Expense with Someone, Group Expenses, My Expenses, and Personal Expense. Internal localization keys such as `sharedSpend`, `groupSplit`, and `splitExpense` remain for compatibility, while their displayed values are localized to the final terminology.

Current terminology risks:

- The internal names `shared_spend` and `split` remain in storage and code even though the user-facing names are clearer.
- “Split” remains valid for actions such as Split equally and Split by amount.
- “Expense with Someone” explains the “friend paid on my behalf” case without implying a cash loan.
- The form must not expose split controls in the default paid-on-behalf path.
- “Borrow” can sound like a cash loan even when the real meaning is “I owe them for a shared purchase.”
- “Udhari” is appropriate for item/service credit and should not be used for an ordinary friend-paid expense unless the product deliberately wants that cultural framing.

## 13. Final product decisions

- The add-menu heading is **What do you want to add?**
- The two-person contact-based expense is **Expense with Someone**.
- Its payer choices are **I paid for them** and **[Person] paid for me**.
- The group feature is **Group Expense** / **Group Expenses**.
- The personal tab is **My Expenses** and its form remains **Personal Expense**.
- Udhari uses the concise title and subtitle: **Udhari** — **Items or services bought or sold on credit.**
- The two underlying storage models remain separate and compatible.

## 14. Safe change checklist

Before modifying an expense/balance feature:

- Decide whether it belongs in `expenses`, `transactions`, or `split_*` tables.
- Decide whether it affects a contact balance.
- Define what the saved `amount` means for the counterparty and for the user.
- Check whether the record needs a source link and whether it is generated or user-authored.
- Update Home ledger filters and contact summaries.
- Update transaction list/detail rendering and category colors.
- Update settlement calculations and history behavior.
- Update export/import and database migrations.
- Update English, Hindi, and Marathi localization strings.
- Check edit/delete behavior, especially for generated split transactions.
- Add or update tests for the balance calculation and important UI/domain cases.
